# Phase 10: Business

Contents: goal · the numbers · money and obligations · operations · one
growth lever · funding · quarterly review · challenges · gate

**Goal:** the product pays its way and runs on a rhythm one person can keep.
A shipped app is a product; it becomes a business when the money is counted,
the obligations are met, and growth is a decision instead of a hope.

Nothing here is legal or tax advice. Rules differ by country and change;
your job is to turn each topic into the short list of questions the founder
takes to an accountant, a lawyer or the official source, and to record the
answers in `RESEARCH.md` with the source and date.

## The numbers (write them in `VENTURE.md` under *Unit economics*, monthly)

| Number | Computed as | Tells the founder |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Net revenue | Receipts minus store or processor fees, refunds and sales tax | What actually arrives |
| Cost to serve | Infrastructure + per-user costs (AI calls, SMS, storage) + tools | Whether each user is worth having |
| Gross margin | (Net revenue − cost to serve) ÷ net revenue | Whether growth helps or hurts |
| Churn | Paying users lost in the month ÷ paying users at its start | How leaky the bucket is |
| Lifetime value | Net revenue per paying user per month ÷ monthly churn | The ceiling for acquisition spend |
| Acquisition cost | Channel spend, including the founder's hours at a rate they set, ÷ new paying users | What a customer costs, per channel |
| Runway | Cash the founder set aside ÷ monthly net burn | The date the kill criteria become urgent |

Compare each number to its own trend and to the category figures already in
`RESEARCH.md`. Do not quote a benchmark from memory. With fewer than about
thirty paying users, read the numbers as direction, not as fact, and say so.

## Money and obligations (ask what is in place; one line each in `VENTURE.md`)

- **Who the seller is.** Selling as a private person, a sole trader or a
  company changes liability, tax and what the stores publish about the
  founder. Ask the country first; everything else depends on it.
- **Sales tax and VAT.** A store or a merchant of record collects and remits
  for the founder; a checkout the founder runs themselves makes it their
  duty in every country they sell to. Know which case applies per revenue
  stream.
- **Separate accounts and books from the first payment.** A business bank
  account and bookkeeping that the founder can keep up in under an hour a
  month, or a bookkeeper.
- **Terms, privacy policy, refunds.** Current, matching what the product does
  today, with a named contact.
- **The name.** Register the trademark once revenue makes the name worth
  defending; before that the phase 5 clearance is enough.
- **Bus factor.** Password manager, recovery codes, domain and store accounts
  on an address that outlives a phone, backups restored once as a test, and a
  note someone trusted could follow if the founder is unavailable.

## Operations (the business must fit the founder's weekly hours)

- One support inbox, saved replies for the five commonest questions, and a
  response target the founder can meet on a bad week.
- A release rhythm (for example every two weeks) instead of releases whenever
  something is ready; a fixed slot for dependency and platform updates.
- An incident routine written before the first incident: how the founder
  finds out, the kill switch from phase 8, where users are told.
- Automate a task the third time it is done by hand. Pay for help when a task
  is both recurring and outside the founder's edge.
- Track the founder's hours for four weeks. If operations take more than the
  budget from phase 1, cut scope before adding features.

## One growth lever per quarter

Pull them in this order; each one makes the next cheaper.

1. **Retention.** Fix the first session and the first week. Talk to churned
   users.
2. **Pricing.** One experiment at a time: a higher price for new users, an
   annual plan, a narrower free tier. Existing users keep their price unless
   the founder decides otherwise.
3. **The working channel.** More of what already brings paying users, until
   its cost per user climbs.
4. **A second channel.** Only when the first is saturating, with the same
   100-user plan discipline as phase 5.
5. **Expansion.** A second platform, a neighbouring segment or a higher
   tier, each entering through phase 2 as its own validation with its own
   kill criteria.

## Funding

Bootstrapping is the default for a one-person venture. Outside money fits
when a tested channel returns more than it costs and cash is the only thing
limiting it, or when the product needs capital before any revenue is
possible. Before the founder raises, ask what the money buys that time would
not, what the investor expects back and by when, and whether the founder
still wants this venture at that size.

## Quarterly review (60 minutes, logged in `LOG.md`)

Read the numbers against the 12-month target and the kill criteria. Then put
one decision to the founder:

- `Go`: grow. Name the one lever for the quarter with a number and a date.
- `Park`: hold. The venture pays for itself at low effort; cap the hours and
  stop investing.
- `Pivot`: the business works for a different segment, price or channel than
  planned. Return to the earliest phase that invalidates.
- `Kill`: sell or sunset. A product with paying users can be sold; list it or
  ask competitors before shutting down. Sunset plan as in phase 9.

Rewrite the 12-month target and the kill criteria for the year ahead at
every fourth review.

## The angel's challenges

- Revenue is not profit, and profit is not a wage. What does the founder
  take home per hour worked?
- A business with one channel, one platform or one large customer has a
  single point of failure. Name it and what would replace it.
- Growth with churn above the category's is paying to fill a leaking bucket.
- If the founder dreads the weekly ritual, the venture is costing more than
  the spreadsheet shows. Ask.

## Gate 10

`Go` when the unit economics are written from real receipts, every item
under *Money and obligations* is answered or has an owner and a date,
operations fit the weekly hours, and the quarter's one lever is chosen. The
gate repeats at each quarterly review.
