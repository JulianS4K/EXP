# Phases 1 and 2: Idea and Validation

Contents: Phase 1 Idea (goal, questions, write-down, challenges, gate) ·
Phase 2 Validation (evidence menu, bar first, interview rules, subagents, gate)

Phase 1 fits in one session and needs no research. Phase 2 needs the
founder to go talk to people; your job is to make that cheap and conclusive.

## Phase 1: Idea

**Goal:** a sentence a stranger understands, one target segment, the problem
in the user's words, why now, the founder's edge, what the founder is
willing to put in, and what would kill it.

### Questions, broad to narrow

First round: the user and the problem. Ask these two before anything else
and before judging anything.

1. Who do they picture using it? Offer two to four candidate segments as
   options, your best guess first, and let them name their own.
2. What is the itch? What happened the last time that person had the
   problem, how do they cope today, and what does coping cost them (time,
   money, embarrassment)? Ask this one open, in the founder's own words;
   options here would put words in their mouth.

Second round: the founder.

3. Why the founder: skills, access to the users, unfair knowledge?
4. What does success look like in 12 months: side income, a living, a
   company to grow or sell, a learning?
5. What goes in: hours per week, money the founder can lose without regret,
   and the date by which it must show something?

Narrow (one decision per unknown, options with consequences):

- One segment first. "Everyone" and "busy people" are not segments; "freelance
  photographers who invoice monthly" is.
- One job the product does for that segment. The rest goes to "Not now".
- A platform guess (confirmed in phase 4), only because it shapes validation.

### Write to `VENTURE.md`

- **One-liner:** `<product> helps <who> <do what> without <pain>`.
- **Target user**, **Problem** (quote a real person if the founder has one),
  **Why now**, **Founder edge**, **Success in 12 months** (a number and a date).
- **Budget:** hours per week, cash at risk, and the review date. Every later
  phase sizes its work to this; a plan that needs more than the budget is a
  different venture.
- **Glossary:** the words the target users use for the problem and its
  parts, one line each, added to as interviews bring more. Use these words in
  questions, copy and code; a term the users would not say does not go in.
- **Kill criteria:** at most three, each measurable and dated with a real
  date inside the budget's review date. Examples: "Fewer than 3 of 10
  interviewees did something about this problem in the last month, by
  <date two weeks out>." "Landing-page signup below 3% from 300 relevant
  visitors, by <date four weeks out>." "No path to the 12-month target with
  realistic conversion, found in phase 3."

### The angel's challenges

- Painkiller or vitamin? What did the user do *last time* this hurt?
- Is the founder the user? If not, who have they actually talked to?
- Is this a product or a feature of something people already have open?
- Can one person build *and* market this to a first release in roughly three
  months, inside the budget just written? If not, what is the smaller
  version that can?
- Who pays, and are they the same person as the user? If nobody pays, what
  is the business?
- What was tried before, by the founder or by others, and why did it stop?

### Gate 1

`Go` when the one-liner, segment, problem, budget, kill criteria and
12-month target are written. `Park` or `Kill` when the founder cannot name one specific person
who has the problem. Record the decision, then recommend a fresh session for
phase 2.

## Phase 2: Validation

**Goal:** evidence that the problem is real, felt, and that the people who
have it can be reached, before anything is built.

### Set the bar before collecting

Ask the founder what number would convince them, and write it in
`VENTURE.md` under *Validation bar* before the first interview or visitor.
Evidence collected without a bar gets rationalised.

### Minimum viable evidence (pick the cheapest that fits, one or two)

| Method | Good when | Bar example | Time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 to 10 problem interviews | Founder can reach the segment | 6 of 10 acted on the problem in the last month | 1 week |
| Smoke test: landing page + one channel | Segment is online and reachable by ads or communities | 3% email signup from 300 relevant visitors | 1 week |
| Demand signals: store reviews, forums, search volume | Competitors exist | 20 complaints about the same gap, from 3 sources | 2 days |
| Pre-sale or paid waitlist | Founder has an audience | 10 payments or 25 waitlist signups with price shown | 1 week |

### Interview rules (hand these to the founder)

- Ask about the past, not the future: "When did this last happen? What did
  you do?" not "Would you use an app that…".
- Never pitch. If they ask what you're building, answer after the interview.
- Compliments are not data. Specifics, money spent, and workarounds are.
- Friends and family count only if they are in the segment and still get the
  past-behaviour questions.
- Write three lines per interview into `RESEARCH.md`: who, what they do today,
  the strongest quote.

### Subagents (see `references/subagents.md`)

- `review-miner` on the top 3 competitor apps: recurring complaints with quotes.
- `demand-scout`: where the segment talks about the problem, with links.

### Gate 2

Compare evidence to the bar. `Go` when met. `Pivot` when the problem is real
but the solution or segment is wrong: write which, and return to phase 1 for
the part that changed. `Kill` when the bar was missed and the founder cannot
name a cheaper test that could still pass. `Park` when reaching the segment
is the blocker. Record the verdict and the evidence lines it rests on.
