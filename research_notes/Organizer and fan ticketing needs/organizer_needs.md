# What event organizers, promoters, venues and festivals want from a ticketing platform (2023–2026, US/NYC emphasis, UK/EU where best)

Method note: the sandbox proxy blocked direct fetches of almost every relevant domain (eventbrite.com, billboard.com, tickpick.com, hi.events, eventcloud.io). Most findings below come from search-result snippets, cited to the URL the snippet came from. Many fee comparisons come from **competitor-authored comparison pages** (hi.events, TickPick, Ticket Fairy, Seatfun, TixFox, TicketsCandy, EventCloud). They are useful for numbers, but they have a motive to make incumbents look expensive. Those are flagged "[vendor-comparison]". I found **no rigorous, independent, published survey that ranks organizers' platform-selection criteria with percentages.** The ranking below is inferred from triangulating reports, pricing and payout policies, complaint themes and regulatory activity.

## 1. What organizers rank as most important when choosing or switching platforms

### Takeaway
The evidence consistently points to this order: (1) total fees and who pays them, (2) payout speed and cash flow, (3) marketing and audience growth plus ownership of buyer data, (4) branding and control, (5) support and reliability (including the door), and then (6) pricing tools, promoter tools, resale control and reporting. Eventbrite's churn reasons match this list almost exactly: fees, branding, slow payouts, support and rigid templates. No independent survey quantifies the ranking.

### Cited Findings
- Common selection criteria listed in buyer guides are ease of use, pricing, customization, integration with marketing tools, customer support and reporting. "Fast payouts, branded ticket pages, and easy setup are common features buyers prioritize." White-label (custom domain, no third-party branding) is described as "in high demand" in 2025. — [Whova](https://whova.com/blog/event-ticketing-software/); [Tixr creators guide 2026](https://creators.tixr.com/post/how-to-choose-a-ticketing-platform) (search snippet aggregated across these guides; not survey data)
- Reasons organizers give for leaving Eventbrite: "high fees that eat into revenue margins, limited branding control that forces their logo and other event advertisements alongside yours, slow payouts that make cash flow difficult, a lack of customer support, and rigid templates". — [TicketSpice](https://www.ticketspice.com/blog/5-alternatives-to-eventbrite) / [TixFox](https://tixfox.co/blog/eventbrite-reviews) [vendor-comparison]
- The complaint is about unpredictability as much as the level: fees "compound and surprise people who didn't model the full cost before launch". Payout timing "catches people off guard" (paid about 3 days after the event, plus up to 5 business days for the bank, so money often lands more than a week after the event). — [Comus Eventbrite reviews summary 2026](https://comus.io/event-management/eventbrite-reviews-summary-fees-payouts/) / [TixFox](https://tixfox.co/blog/eventbrite-reviews) [vendor-comparison]
- Pricing instability drives churn risk. Eventbrite has reportedly raised pricing "11 increases since 2007", with major restructurings in 2023 and 2025, and was reported acquired by Bending Spoons for about $500M in Dec 2025. — [TicketSpice](https://www.ticketspice.com/blog/5-alternatives-to-eventbrite) [vendor-comparison; the acquisition should be verified against primary news]
- Audience control is framed as the item "most promoters end up caring about most". Neither DICE nor Eventbrite "is built primarily around organizer control of the audience". — [Seatfun, DICE vs Eventbrite for music venues 2026](https://www.seatfun.com/blog/dice-vs-eventbrite) [vendor-comparison]
- Opacity is a pain point for small organizers. Tixr and DICE do not publish fee tables; "the small organizer pays the price of having no leverage… you can't compare offers you have to book a call to see". Payout timing is also negotiated by contract. — [TickPick Organizer: Dice vs Posh vs Tixr vs TickPick 2026](https://www.tickpick.com/blog/organizer/dice-vs-posh-vs-tixr-vs-tickpick) [vendor-comparison]
- Regulation now shapes what organizers want. The FTC junk-fees rule (in effect May 2025) requires all-in pricing for live-event tickets, so organizers need platforms that show the total price upfront. — [Whova / buyer guides](https://whova.com/blog/event-ticketing-software/); NIVA's report also references the FTC all-in rule — [NIVA State of Live 2025 PDF](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/NIVA-State-of-Live-2025-Report.pdf) (note: one snippet said "May 2024". The FTC final rule took effect May 12, 2025.)
- Security and trust: "ticket fraud and resale abuse can erode credibility fast". — [buyer guide snippet, Whova/InEvent](https://inevent.com/blog/events/ticketing-platforms.html)

### Inferences
- The consistent churn pattern is fees, then cash flow, then control (brand and data), then support. A platform that is transparent (published fees, buyer-pays all-in), pays fast and hands over the buyer list meets the top three needs at once.
- "Ease of setup" is table stakes. It separates self-serve tools (Eventbrite, Posh, Ticket Tailor) from sales-led ones (DICE, Tixr, AXS), but it is rarely cited as a reason to switch.

### Gaps
- No quantified ranking (for example "% of organizers who say fees are the #1 factor") from an independent survey. Eventbrite, Posh, DICE and Shotgun do not publish organizer "why we chose / why we left" statistics that I could find.
- G2 and Capterra pages could not be fetched, so aggregate review scores and theme counts are missing.

## 2. Surveys and industry reports: concrete numbers

### Takeaway
Available organizer surveys (Eventbrite TRNDS, NIVA State of Live, NITO NY fee study, AIF/Skiddle, NTIA) cover sentiment, economics and fees, not platform preferences. The most decision-relevant numbers:
- 64% of US independent stages were unprofitable in 2024 (81% in NY).
- The average NY primary ticket fee is 28.65%, and 34.7% on Ticketmaster, TicketWeb and AXS.
- 8 in 10 organizers say building community around events matters.
- UK split-payment usage rose 48%.

### Cited Findings
**Eventbrite TRNDS 2025 (US, global organizer + attendee survey plus Eventbrite ticketing data)**
- 71% of organizers feel optimistic about 2025. 8 in 10 organizers believe it's important to grow communities around their events. 63% think consumers want more micro-events and intimate gatherings. — [Eventbrite TRNDS 2025 PDF](https://www.eventbrite.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Eventbrite-TRNDS-2025-US.pdf); [Eventbrite TRNDS page](https://www.eventbrite.com/blog/academy/event-trends-report-2023/)
- Consumer side: 76% plan to spend the same or more on live experiences, and 30% of Gen Z use TikTok to discover live experiences. — [Eventbrite TRNDS 2025 PDF](https://www.eventbrite.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Eventbrite-TRNDS-2025-US.pdf)
- Eventbrite's 2026 successor is the "Social Study" report on Gen Z and Millennials ("Reset to Real"). — [Exhibitor Online](https://www.exhibitoronline.com/news/article.asp?ID=24901); [Eventbrite event trends](https://www.eventbrite.com/l/event-trends/)
- Older data (2023): US organizers forecast their top challenges as insufficient budget (32%), reaching attendees (27%), increased competition (23%) and ticket sales (21%). — [Eventbrite event statistics](https://www.eventbrite.com/blog/event-statistics-ds00/) [2023 data, flagged]

**NIVA "State of Live" 2025 (US independent venues and promoters, 2024 data, by Teconomy Partners)**
- 64% of independent stages were unprofitable in 2024. By state: NY 81%, OH 80%, CA 69%, FL 65%. — [Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/2025/06/23/nivas-state-of-live-survey-finds-independent-venues-generated-153-1b-in-total-economic-output-across-u-s-in-2024-64-stages-struggling-with-unprofitability/); [Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/pro/niva-state-of-live-report-indie-music-venues-unprofitable/)
- Nearly half reported decreased revenue. More than two-thirds saw higher operating costs. 31% of expenses went to artist/booking fees and 26% to staffing. The sector produced $153.1B in total output and $86.2B in GDP. — [NIVA State of Live 2025 PDF](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/NIVA-State-of-Live-2025-Report.pdf); [Music Ally](https://musically.com/2025/06/24/report-lays-bare-struggles-of-us-independent-live-music-business/)
- NIVA frames large ticketing and resale platforms as "playing unfairly, hurting both fan trust and the revenue that venues depend on". Its "Fix the Tix" campaign pushes resale caps. — [NIVA PDF](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/NIVA-State-of-Live-2025-Report.pdf); [NIVA Fix the Tix](https://www.nivassoc.org/fixthetix)

**NITO (National Independent Talent Organization) NY Ticket Fees Report, Oct 2025**
- 86 NY venues studied. The average primary ticket fee is 28.65% of face value, up more than 36% since the state's 2016 study. — [Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/2025/10/22/finding-a-crisis-nito-calls-for-ny-to-cap-ticket-fees/); [Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/music/music-industry-news/new-york-concert-ticket-fees-study-1236405679/)
- Ticketmaster, TicketWeb and AXS averaged 34.7% of face value, nearly double all other providers (18.7%). 82 of the 86 instances where fees exceeded 40% of face value were on Ticketmaster, TicketWeb or AXS. — [NITO report PDF](https://nitolive.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/NITO-NY-Ticket-Fees-Report-.pdf); [NYS Music](https://nysmusic.com/2025/10/23/nito-report-exposes-the-surge-in-ticket-fees-in-new-york-state/)
- Independent venues generally had lower fees. Example: No Fun (Troy, NY) on Eventbrite had fees under $2 on every ticket studied. NITO asks NY to cap fees at 15% of face value. — [NYS Music](https://nysmusic.com/2025/10/23/nito-report-exposes-the-surge-in-ticket-fees-in-new-york-state/)

**UK: AIF/Skiddle, NTIA**
- Skiddle and AIF Festival Report (2024 season): more than 1.8M festival tickets sold via Skiddle, and split-payment (instalment) usage rose 48%. — [Festival Insights](https://www.festivalinsights.com/2025/04/skiddle-association-independent-festivals-release-festival-report/)
- 172 independent UK festivals have closed since 2019, amid about 44% production-cost inflation (per aggregator). — [VoxBooster festival stats](https://voxbooster.com/blog/music-festival-statistics-2026/) [secondary aggregator, flagged]
- NTIA: in a flash survey of more than 500 UK nightlife businesses, 90% have cut or plan to cut staff, hours or investment, and 40% expect to close within 6 months without relief. 50% of venues expect business rates to rise 50% or more in 2026. NTIA also warned of "public scepticism towards dynamic ticket pricing". — [NTIA research](https://ntia.co.uk/research-and-reports/); [Morning Advertiser](https://www.morningadvertiser.co.uk/Article/2025/12/19/half-of-night-time-venues-expect-business-rates-to-rise-by-more-than-50-percent-in-2026/); [NTIA on dynamic pricing](https://ntia.co.uk/ntia-says-public-scepticism-towards-dynamic-ticket-pricing-should-be-a-wake-up-call-for-the-live-events-industry/)
- NTIA/Obsurvant survey of 2,001 UK 18–30-year-olds: going out less, citing finances, safety and transport. — [Nightlife International](https://www.nightlifeinternational.org/en/news/item/4648-ntia-uk-releases-key-2025-reports-on-the-night-time-economy-and-electronic-music-industry)

### Inferences
- Independent operators are margin-starved: 64% unprofitable nationally, 81% in NY. That makes both fee level and cash-flow timing existential, not just nice-to-have.
- In NY, the fee gap between enterprise platforms (~35%) and independent-friendly ones (~19%) is a concrete positioning wedge for an NYC-focused platform.
- Instalments ("split payment") are a proven lever for festival conversion in the UK.

### Gaps
- DICE, Posh, Shotgun and Tixr do not appear to publish organizer surveys with percentages. Pollstar's 2025 Year-End Executive Survey exists ([Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/2025/12/28/pollstars-2025-year-end-executive-survey/)), but I couldn't retrieve its ticketing-specific numbers.
- There is no NYC-specific promoter survey on platform preference.

## 3. Fee structures and payout schedules compared

### Takeaway
The market splits three ways:
- **Buyer-pays percentage models:** Eventbrite 3.7% + $1.79 + 2.9% processing; Posh 10% + $0.99, buyer-paid since Apr 2026; Humanitix 5% + $1.29; RA about 10%.
- **Organizer-absorbed or negotiated all-in models:** DICE about 10%, Tixr and AXS negotiated.
- **Flat per-ticket SaaS:** Ticket Tailor $0.85 down to $0.30.

Payouts range from instant or same-night (Posh, KYD, Stripe-direct tools) to post-event with reserves (Eventbrite: 20% reserve, $100k pre-event cap; DICE: within 5 business days post-event with up to 5% held for 6 months).

### Inferences
- The typical all-in cost for a small promoter on a buyer-pays platform is about 10–14% of a $25–40 ticket. Absorbed models (DICE) move the same ~10% onto the organizer.
- Posh's April 2026 switch to buyer-pays all-in shows that organizers prefer keeping their full target payout.
- Payout speed is a paid feature on most incumbents: Eventbrite charges 3% for instant payouts, and DICE holds 5% for 6 months. Instant or near-instant payouts at no extra cost (Posh, KYD, Stripe-direct) is a real differentiator for cash-strapped indies.

### Gaps
- Official Shotgun, See Tickets, AXS and DICE US fee schedules could not be retrieved or are unpublished.
- RA payout timing is unknown.
- Pollstar/IQ reporting on the size of ticketing advances for festivals (2024–26) wasn't found.

## 4. Small/independent promoters vs larger venues and festivals

### Takeaway
Small promoters and nightlife operators prioritize:
- instant payouts
- social/Instagram- and TikTok-driven discovery
- promoter and affiliate links with commissions
- guest lists
- SMS
- simple self-serve setup with published pricing

Venues and festivals prioritize:
- negotiated all-in deals
- advances
- reserved seating
- reliable high-throughput (offline-capable) door scanning
- resale control and data
- instalment payments

### Cited Findings
- Posh positions itself for "nightlife, comedy, supper clubs, brunches, mixers, and culture-led events". It includes native SMS opt-in at checkout and pushes keyword/shortcode SMS list building because "nightlife is a same-day decision… email never gets opened in time". — [Posh University SMS](https://posh.vip/university/post/best-way-to-sell-event-tickets-sms-marketing)
- Posh "Kickback" is a built-in affiliate/referral system. Public offers are open to anyone; private offers go to chosen promoters. Posh takes a 20% fee on the kickback amount (a $10 offer pays the affiliate $7). — [Posh support](https://support.posh.vip/en/articles/10723700-creating-kickback-affiliate-offers-public-private); [Posh docs](https://docs.posh.vip/kickback)
- The nightlife SMS tooling category also covers managing guest lists, tracking promoter performance and VIP contacts, with same-day text blasts backing weekly email. — [EZ Texting](https://www.eztexting.com/industries/bars-clubs-nightlife); [Club Texting thought-leader series](https://www.clubtexting.com/thought-leader-series-how-successful-nightlife-promoters-use-text-messaging-marketing)
- Discovery is social: 30% of Gen Z use TikTok to discover live experiences. — [Eventbrite TRNDS 2025](https://www.eventbrite.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Eventbrite-TRNDS-2025-US.pdf)
- Independent organizers "almost always do better on self-serve flat-fee tools for rooms they control". Marketplace platforms (Universe, TicketSource) can add incremental buyers. — [hi.events TicketSource vs Universe](https://hi.events/compare/ticketsource-vs-universe) [vendor-comparison]
- Tixr Studio offers rule-based dynamic pricing (inventory thresholds, time windows, sales velocity). This is aimed at larger promoters and festivals. — [Tixr Studio](https://creators.tixr.com/products/studio)
- Door reliability: venue Wi-Fi and cell networks fail when thousands of phones connect. A gate failure cascades into unverified tickets, unrecorded walk-ups and broken reconciliation. Offline scanning with duplicate detection and sync is the expected answer. — [Fastbreak](https://www.fastbreak.ai/blog/fastbreak-ticketing-offline-gate-scanning); [DEV Community](https://dev.to/animesh_gupta_705a19fc6f8/designing-event-qr-check-in-that-works-when-the-venues-wi-fi-doesnt-i1l); [Eventleash](https://www.eventleash.com/en/blog/offline-event-check-in-software)
- Known offline edge case: the same QR can be admitted at multiple scanners inside the sync window, and without connectivity that window is unbounded (a crowd-safety issue). — [GitHub issue, WoCo Event App #641](https://github.com/yea-80y/WoCo-Event-App/issues/641)
- NYC venue evidence: KYD Labs runs Le Poisson Rouge and The Brooklyn Monarch (1,000+ shows a year, ~600 touring artists). It reports a 30% sales jump at LPR after onboarding, and pitches "audience ownership, loyalty, and ticket resale" as making events "2x more profitable". — [KYD Labs](https://kydlabs.com/about); [aVenture](https://aventure.vc/companies/kyd-labs-new-york-city-ny-us) [company claims, not independently verified]
- Festivals and UK: instalments (split payment, up 48% on Skiddle) and cost pressure matter. 1 in 5 independent festivals has reportedly closed since 2023. — [Festival Insights](https://www.festivalinsights.com/2025/04/skiddle-association-independent-festivals-release-festival-report/); [VoxBooster](https://voxbooster.com/blog/music-festival-statistics-2026/)
- DICE says customer data is owned by the Event Partners. It acts as an agent, and organizers are the data controllers who can contact ticket holders. — [DICE US privacy policy for event partners](https://dicefm.zendesk.com/hc/en-gb/articles/5764571321873-United-States-Privacy-Policy-for-Event-Partners)

### Inferences
- For NYC nightlife and independent promoters, the combination that wins is instant payouts, promoter links with automatic commission payout, SMS and email capture the organizer owns, guest lists, and published buyer-pays fees. Posh's traction is built on this bundle despite its roughly 10% take.
- Venues and festivals care more about throughput and door reliability (offline-safe with duplicate prevention), financing and advances, resale economics, and negotiated rates.

### Gaps
- Reddit (r/Promoters, r/nightlife, r/EventPlanning) threads were not retrievable through search. There are no first-person quantified promoter anecdotes.
- There are no hard numbers on guest-list share of attendance or promoter-link share of sales.

## 5. Organizer attitudes to secondary/resale (control, price caps, resale revenue share)

### Takeaway
Independent venues, artists and festivals strongly favor face-value caps and platform-controlled resale:
- NIVA "Fix the Tix": cap at original total cost and a 10% fee cap.
- The UK government committed to a face-value cap in Nov 2025.
- NY has a bill to cap concert resale at face value.
- Voters back caps 78%.

Organizers also increasingly want a share of resale revenue (Shotgun 70/30 upgrade split, KYD royalties) and to own resale data.

### Cited Findings
- The "Fix the Tix" coalition of venues, artists and live orgs urged Senate Commerce to amend the TICKET Act to include a federal resale cap at original total cost and a 10% fee cap. — [TicketNews](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/05/irving-azoff-aligned-music-artists-coalition-pushes-survey-backing-resale-caps-at-core-of-industry-lobbying-efforts/); [NIVA Fix the Tix](https://www.nivassoc.org/fixthetix)
- Music Artists Coalition poll (800 registered voters): 78% support a resale price cap, 85% support face-value disclosure. — [TicketNews, May 2026](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/05/irving-azoff-aligned-music-artists-coalition-pushes-survey-backing-resale-caps-at-core-of-industry-lobbying-efforts/)
- NIVA urged US states to replicate the UK's resale price cap and end speculative listings (2025). — [NIVA statement](https://www.nivassoc.org/statements/2025/independent-venues-festivals-call-for-us-states-to-replicate-the-uks-new-ticket-resale-price-cap-policy-and-to-end-speculative-listings)
- UK: in Nov 2025 the government announced a ban on resale above face value (bar a capped admin fee), with platforms required to enforce it. ### Inferences
- For independent and NYC organizers, the resale features that fit their values and the direction of regulation are:
  - native face-value (or capped) resale / waitlist, with an organizer toggle
  - resale revenue share to the organizer
  - resale buyers captured into the organizer's data
  - anti-scalp rotating or day-of barcodes
- Dynamic pricing is wanted by larger promoters but carries reputational risk in nightlife and UK markets. An opt-in, rule-based (tiered) approach is safer than algorithmic surge pricing.

### Gaps
- No survey quantifies the share of organizers who want a resale revenue share or caps. Evidence is advocacy positions and platform features.
- The status of the NY resale-cap bills (passed or pending) as of Sep 2026 was not confirmed.
- Refund and chargeback preferences (who bears chargebacks, refund-policy controls) have only policy-level evidence (Eventbrite: 20% reserve, chargebacks netted from payouts; DICE: 5% holdback for 6 months). There are no organizer survey data.
