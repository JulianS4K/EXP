# Organizer / Promoter / Venue Pain Points and Praise for Ticketing Platforms (2023–2026)

Method note: about 24 tool calls (WebSearch plus a few WebFetch attempts). Billboard, WHYY and TickPick were blocked by the egress proxy, so their content below comes from search-result snippets only. Reddit threads didn't show up directly in search; the Reddit-flavored claims here come via Trustpilot/G2 aggregators and competitor blogs. **Source-bias warning:** many comparison pages (hi.events, Ticket Tailor, TickPick, Posh University, Ticket Fairy, Ticketsauce, SimpleTix, Equaticket, TicketCrest, Promotix) are written by *competitors* who want organizers to switch. Treat them as indicative of the talking points, not as neutral evidence. Evidence strength is tagged as **[pattern]** (many reviews, news, or filings), **[anecdote]** (a single review), or **[vendor claim]** (the platform's own marketing, or a competitor's blog).

## Q1. Per platform: what organizers praise, what they complain about, notable incidents

### Takeaway
Across the set, organizers love two things: low or transparent fees with fast payouts (Ticket Tailor, Posh, KYD) and built-in audience/marketing reach (DICE, Posh, Shotgun, RA, Eventbrite discovery). They hate payout holds and reserves, surprise fee changes, support that disappears at crunch time, and losing control of buyer data or branding (DICE's app and account requirement). The big incidents in the window: Eventbrite's 2023–24 fee and plan churn, followed by its take-private by Bending Spoons (closed March 2026); See Tickets' 2023 checkout-skimming breach ($3.25M settlement); Posh's $1.2M drip-pricing class action; DICE's 2023 layoffs and 2025 sale to Fever; and the Live Nation/Ticketmaster antitrust verdict (April 2026).

**Eventbrite**
- 2023 increases: service fee from 3.5% to 3.7%, per-ticket fee from $1.59 to $1.79, card processing from 2.5% to 2.9% [pattern; secondary blog] — [TicketsCandy](https://blog.ticketscandy.com/eventbrite-fees-exposed-2025/)
- On Aug 3 2023 Eventbrite emailed that new listing plans (Flex / Pro / Premium, per-event or monthly subscription) would take effect Sept 6 2023. Pro ran $29–$159/month by ticket volume. The Flex plan was later discontinued — [Eventbrite Help Center](https://www.eventbrite.com/help/en-us/articles/193833/eventbrites-new-pricing-plans/); [AlwaysTheVIP](https://www.alwaysthevip.com/blog/a-quick-guide-to-eventbrite-pricing-and-fees-2023/)
- Eventbrite then reversed and made publishing free for all event sizes — [Eventbrite blog](https://www.eventbrite.com/blog/free-to-publish-events/). One source describes this as "eliminated organiser fees entirely… ~$20M revenue hit." In fact it removed *listing* fees; per-ticket service fees remain. Treat that wording as inaccurate — [Ticket Tailor blog (competitor)](https://www.tickettailor.com/blog/bending-spoons-acquires-eventbrite-what-does-this-mean)
- Organizers call total fees of 11% or more per ticket "one of the costliest" [vendor claim] — [TicketsCandy](https://blog.ticketscandy.com/eventbrite-fees-exposed-2025/)
- The same source says fees rose "approximately 30% over two years without a commensurate improvement," damaging organizer trust [competitor opinion] — [Ticket Tailor blog](https://www.tickettailor.com/blog/bending-spoons-acquires-eventbrite-what-does-this-mean)
- Ratings split sharply: G2/Capterra 4.3–4.6/5 vs Trustpilot 1.2/5. Payout delays are called "one of the most common complaints from US event organizers," with Eventbrite releasing payouts about 3 days after the event and holding a 20% reserve. One organizer reported payouts showing "cancelled" with no option to reissue [pattern per aggregator; the reserve figure is unverified against primary docs] — [CheckThat.ai](https://checkthat.ai/brands/eventbrite/reviews); [TicketCrest (competitor)](https://ticketcrest.com/blog/eventbrite-holds-your-money); Eventbrite's own docs on delayed/missing payouts: [Help Center](https://www.eventbrite.com/help/en-us/articles/613631/delayed-or-missing-payouts/), [Instant Payouts](https://www.eventbrite.com/help/en-us/articles/360444/get-paid-with-instant-payouts/)
- Paid tickets: 83.8M in 2024, down 10.3% from 2023, and still about 21% below 2019 — [Ticket Tailor blog summarizing filings](https://www.tickettailor.com/blog/bending-spoons-acquires-eventbrite-what-does-this-mean); primary: [Eventbrite FY2024 shareholder letter (SEC)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1475115/000147511525000025/q4shareholderletterfinal.htm)
- Bending Spoons deal: announced Dec 2 2025 (about $500M cash), closed Mar 10 2026. Headcount went from 866 (end-2023) to 636 (end-2025), and further cuts followed the acquisition. MBW reports Eventbrite eyeing secondary ticketing — [MBW](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/eventbrite-eyes-jump-into-secondary-ticketing-market-after-500m-acquisition-by-bending-spoons/); [CMU ("stagnating")](https://completemusicupdate.com/bending-spoons-to-buy-stagnating-ticketing-platform-eventbrite/); [SimpleTix (competitor) on staff cuts](https://www.simpletix.com/eventbrites-new-owner-just-cut-staff-what-it-means-for-event-organizers-in-2026/); [Berenzweig Leonard](https://www.berenzweiglaw.com/blog/2026/03/going-dark-how-eventbrites-billion-dollar-valuation-dwindled/)
- Praise and a switching case: Detroit Auto Show moved to Eventbrite and sold 58,000 paid advance tickets in one week for its 2023 event [vendor claim, in Eventbrite's SEC shareholder letter] — [Eventbrite 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1475115/000147511524000161/shareholderletterq22024f.htm)

**Ticketmaster / Live Nation (incl. Universe) and AXS**
- May 2024: DOJ plus 40 states sued Live Nation over control of venues, promotion and ticketing — [NY AG](https://ag.ny.gov/press-release/2026/attorney-general-james-and-coalition-states-win-trial-against-live-nation-and)
- Trial began Mar 2 2026 in SDNY (Judge Subramanian). DOJ settled mid-trial; terms were published Mar 9 2026. The settlement requires no Ticketmaster divestiture. Instead it has structural changes that let venues use multiple ticketing vendors rather than Ticketmaster exclusively, runs LN-owned amphitheaters as "open venues" with up to 50% of tickets distributable by outside promoters, extends the consent decree by 8 years, and creates a $280M fund for state damages and penalty claims. It needs Tunney Act approval — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_v._Live_Nation_Entertainment); [Live Nation statement](https://news.livenationentertainment.com/statements/live-nation-entertainment-reaches-settlement-with-u-s-department-of-justice); [Variety AU](https://au.variety.com/2026/biz/global/live-nation-reaches-settlement-department-of-justice-34002)
- 33 states plus DC continued. On Apr 15 2026 the jury found LN/TM liable on every count, including monopolizing primary ticketing for major concert venues and illegally tying promotion to venue ticketing. Remedies, possibly including a breakup, are pending — [Paul Weiss](https://www.paulweiss.com/insights/client-memos/live-nationticketmaster-antitrust-verdict-key-takeaways-from-the-states-jury-trial-win); [NH DOJ](https://www.doj.nh.gov/news-and-media/live-nation-and-ticketmaster-antitrust-verdict); [Crowell](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/after-the-verdict-navigating-the-live-nationticketmaster-antitrust-fallout)
- For independent venues, both TM and AXS require enterprise contracts and aren't self-serve. The same source says TM's standard promoter contracts often restrict fan-data access [competitor blog] — [Ticket Fairy](https://www.ticketfairy.com/us/event-ticketing/ticketmaster-vs-axs); [ProTicket](https://proticket.com.ng/ticketmaster-vs-axs-vs-live-nation-promoters-guide-2026/)
- Many NIVA member venues use Ticketmaster under exclusive contracts. Source bias: Chamber of Progress is a tech-industry-funded group with a position — [Chamber of Progress](https://progresschamber.org/research/captured-by-concert-machine-niva-live-nation/)
- Universe (TM's self-serve product): I found no substantive 2023–26 organizer reviews (gap).

**DICE**
- App-based model: tickets live in the app, and the QR code activates near the event. Buyers must create an account, which organizers see as DICE collecting "your" customers' data. Organizers also report limited ownership/control and competing inside DICE's ecosystem instead of building their own audience [pattern per competitor summaries of reviews] — [Ticket Tailor DICE review summary](https://www.tickettailor.com/blog/dice-reviews-summary-pros-cons); [hi.events](https://hi.events/dice-alternative)
- Praise: all-in pricing, anti-scalping, analytics that help promoters decide when and where to book and what to charge — [Trapital](https://www.trapital.com/episodes/how-dice-makes-ticketing-more-fan-focused); [DICE promoters page (vendor)](https://dice.fm/partners/work-with-us/promoters?lng=en-US)
- Described as a curated discovery app "you have to be invited onto" — [TickPick snippet (competitor)](https://www.tickpick.com/blog/organizer/dice-vs-posh-vs-tixr-vs-tickpick)
- Laid off 30+ staff in Aug 2023, with the creative team hit hardest — [RA News](https://ra.co/news/79368); [IQ](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2023/08/ticketing-platform-dice-confirms-layoffs/)
- Acquired by Fever in June 2025, after Fever bought Atom Tickets in March 2025 — [MBW](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/dice-acquired-by-live-events-discovery-platform-fever/); [Music Ally](https://musically.com/2025/06/05/fever-acquires-ticketing-firm-dice-in-live-tech-consolidation/)
- NA office has been at 10 Grand St, Brooklyn since 2021 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dice_(ticketing_company))
- Trustpilot and BBB (Brooklyn) complaint pages exist, mostly from fans — [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/dice.fm?page=3); [BBB](https://bbb.org/us/ny/brooklyn/profile/event-ticket-sales/dice-0121-87172159/complaints)

**Posh**
- Raised a $5M seed in April 2023 — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/04/27/event-management-ticketing-platform-posh-raises-5-million-seed-round)
- Positioned as the fastest-growing NYC nightlife platform [vendor claim] — [Posh University](https://posh.vip/university/post/best-ticketing-app-nyc-events)
- Payouts: sales become available 24–48 business hours after purchase with free standard payout; instant payout costs 5% — [TickPick snippet](https://www.tickpick.com/blog/organizer/ticketing-platform-payout-schedules)
- Fees reported as 10% + $0.99 (buyer-paid); another source puts them at 5–10% + a per-order fee — [TickPick snippet](https://www.tickpick.com/blog/organizer/dice-vs-posh-vs-tixr-vs-tickpick); [businessmodelcanvastemplate](https://businessmodelcanvastemplate.com/blogs/how-it-works/posh-how-it-works)
- Praise: free unlimited SMS blasts, promoter "Kickbacks" and tracked links, ad tracking, supportive support team [G2 pattern] — [G2](https://www.g2.com/products/posh-posh/reviews); [Posh platform](https://posh.vip/platform)
- Complaint: one organizer on Trustpilot said the system "collapsed," support was unresponsive, and they lost thousands [anecdote] — [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/posh.vip)
- Dedicated SMS routing costs $19.99/month, non-refundable — [Posh KB](https://support.posh.vip/en/articles/15089436-sms-campaigns-messaging-guidelines-best-practices)
- $1.2M class settlement (Doctor v. Posh Group) over fees not included in the advertised price for California buyers — [ClassAction.org](https://www.classaction.org/news/1.2m-posh-group-settlement-ends-class-action-lawsuit-over-alleged-failure-to-disclose-additional-fees)

**See Tickets**
- Checkout skimmer ran Feb 28–Jul 2 2023 and captured name, address, full card number and CVV for 323k+ people. Notice was filed with the Maine AG on Sept 6 2023. A $3.25M class settlement was preliminarily approved June 20 2024. See Tickets had an earlier 2019–2021 skimming breach (pre-window, from memory, not re-verified here) — [ClassAction.org](https://www.classaction.org/news/see-tickets-hit-with-class-action-over-months-long-2023-data-breach); [Top Class Actions](https://topclassactions.com/lawsuit-settlements/closed-settlements/3-25m-see-tickets-data-breach-class-action-settlement/)

**Shotgun**
- Started in 2014 in Paris's underground electronic scene, now sells in 49 countries. Payment volume rose 560%+ on Stripe Connect. Claims 5M+ monthly fans "tuned into your genre and location" [vendor claim] — [Stripe case study](https://stripe.com/customers/shotgun); [Shotgun Pro](https://pro.shotgun.live/en)

**Tixr**
- Praised for table/bottle/VIP maps and upsells, and for treating ticketing as the start of a fan relationship [blog/competitor] — [Ticket Fairy](https://www.ticketfairy.com/event-ticketing/eventbrite-vs-tixr); [Respect My Region](https://respectmyregion.com/tixr-ticketing-platform-for-live-events-2025/)

**RA Tickets**
- Launched in 2008 for electronic-music promoters — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Advisor)
- RA is "gradually introducing service fees," so not all promoters can view tax invoices yet — [RA support](https://support.ra.co/article/178-tax-invoices); [RA ticket fees](https://support.ra.co/article/177-service-fees)

**Humanitix**
- US fee is 5% + $1.29, or 3.9% + $1.29 for charities, schools and nonprofits. Net profits go to education charities. The fee is passed to the buyer by default — [Humanitix pricing](https://humanitix.com/us/pricing); [Help Centre](https://help.humanitix.com/en/articles/8896957-not-for-profit-nfp-charity-and-school-rate)
- Pays out on its own schedule rather than in real time, a cash-flow problem for organizers who owe venue deposits [competitor blog] — [Promotix](https://blog.promotix.com/humanitix-review)

**Ticket Tailor**
- Rated 4.8/5 on Capterra across 2,100+ reviews. Reviewers cite the flat fee, instant payouts, white-label branding, and fast support as reasons they left Eventbrite [pattern] — [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/112510/Ticket-Tailor/reviews/?page=2); [Toolradar](https://toolradar.com/tools/ticket-tailor)

**KYD Labs (NYC)**
- Founded 2022 in NYC. Raised a $7M seed (a16z crypto, Techstars) and runs on Solana. Claims 300k+ tickets, $10M+ in sales, $2M+ in venue financing originated, same-day payouts, 0% merchant fees and resale royalties [vendor claims] — [KYD Labs](https://kydlabs.com/); [Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/kyd-labs/__F1wPEr7ms4Pk4Azq-pFD3--coXC8NWapxED59WmAhUo)
- Launched TIX, a DeFi event-financing and settlement network, in Dec 2025 — [TicketNews](https://www.ticketnews.com/2025/12/founders-kyd-labs-launch-tix/)
- Pitch talk title: "NFT ticketing doesn't work and we're selling to Ticketmaster" — [Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/learn/breakpoint-25/nft-ticketing-doesnt-work-and-were-selling-to-ticketmaster-kyd-labs)

### Inferences
- Eventbrite's churn in listing plans between 2023 and 2024 (introduced, then partly reversed within about a year), followed by its sale to a cost-cutting acquirer, is the clearest "fee-change trust shock" in the set. Bending Spoons' known playbook of raising monetization makes further organizer-side fee changes plausible (speculative).
- DICE's pain points (app-only access, forced account, data ownership) are the flip side of its strengths (anti-scalping, discovery). Its sale to Fever adds consolidation risk for promoters.
- Posh's drip-pricing settlement shows that buyer-paid fee models create legal exposure. All-in or transparent pricing is now partly a compliance issue: the FTC junk-fee rule for live events took effect May 2025 (context, not verified in this session).

### Gaps
- There are no direct Reddit threads (r/Promoters, r/nightlife) in the results. Search didn't surface them and Reddit fetch wasn't attempted, so the "Reddit sentiment" claims are indirect.
- I found no primary-source confirmation of Eventbrite's "20% reserve" figure.
- Universe, AXS (organizer side), RA, Shotgun and Tixr have no negative organizer evidence found. That is absence of results, not absence of complaints.
- I didn't verify the precise terms of the pending remedy phase after the April 2026 verdict.

## Q2. Why organizers switch platforms, and case studies

### Takeaway
Stated reasons for switching are: fees (especially Eventbrite's 2023 hikes), payout speed and cash flow (holds, reserves, post-event payouts), branding and data control, niche features (tables/VIP for nightlife, promoter tracking), and, at venue scale, advances and signing bonuses. Documented named case studies are scarce and mostly vendor-authored.

### Cited Findings
- Ticket Tailor reviewers say the flat fee, instant payouts and white-label branding are why they left Eventbrite [pattern, aggregated reviews] — [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/112510/Ticket-Tailor/reviews/?page=4)
- Detroit Auto Show switched to Eventbrite (sold 58k advance tickets in one week for 2023) [vendor claim] — [Eventbrite SEC letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1475115/000147511524000161/shareholderletterq22024f.htm)
- GA ticketing for clubs, theaters and festivals has "never been more competitive," with more than a dozen companies (Eventbrite, Tixr, Etix, VenuePilot…) vying. TM, AXS, SeatGeek, See Tickets and others compete aggressively with advances — [Billboard (snippet)](https://www.billboard.com/pro/ticketmaster-alternatives-ticketing-company-competitors/)
- Critique of the advance model: signing bonuses used to "run the ticketing business"; ticketers "should not act like banks"; the financing raises long-term costs [opinion] — [Joseph Stern, Medium](https://josephiswhere.medium.com/signing-bonuses-used-to-run-the-ticketing-business-what-happens-now-275073a7533e); [Justin Kantor Substack](https://justinkantor.substack.com/p/the-high-cost-of-free-money)
- Large promoters negotiate advances against projected sales in exchange for volume and exclusivity commitments — same sources as above
- DOJ settlement terms (multi-vendor ticketing at venues, open amphitheaters) could loosen exclusivity and create switching opportunities for venues — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_v._Live_Nation_Entertainment)
- Eventbrite is "stagnating": paid tickets were down 10.3% in 2024, which implies organizer churn — [CMU](https://completemusicupdate.com/bending-spoons-to-buy-stagnating-ticketing-platform-eventbrite/)

### Inferences
- Organizers leaving Eventbrite split two ways: cost-sensitive, community and nonprofit events go to Ticket Tailor or Humanitix, and nightlife goes to Posh, DICE, Tixr or Shotgun.
- Venue-level switching is driven by advances and contract terms more than by product.

### Gaps
- I found no verifiable named NYC venue case studies of moving from Eventbrite to DICE, Tixr or Posh. Billboard's detailed article was blocked.

## Q3. What NYC nightlife and club promoters specifically complain about

### Takeaway
There's little direct, citable NYC-specific promoter testimony. Available evidence points to fee levels (Posh at about 10% + $0.99), DICE's app requirement and data control, reliability and support at the door, and the rise of informal channels (Partiful, group chats) competing with ticketing platforms.

### Cited Findings
- Posh is described as the strongest-growing NYC nightlife platform, used for day parties, club nights and supper clubs [vendor claim] — [Posh University](https://posh.vip/university/post/best-ticketing-app-nyc-events)
- Posh's 10% + $0.99 fee is "roughly double" the 5% alternative, for margin-focused promoters [competitor claim] — [TickPick snippet](https://www.tickpick.com/blog/organizer/dice-vs-posh-vs-tixr-vs-tickpick)
- DICE's app-only access and account requirement frustrate organizers and fans — [Ticket Tailor](https://www.tickettailor.com/blog/dice-reviews-summary-pros-cons)
- Partiful doesn't publish a fixed fee rate, so organizers have to run a test event to see it [competitor] — [hi.events](https://hi.events/compare/dice-vs-posh)
- The Secondary newsletter frames informal group-chat sales as a "$37M problem" for ticketing — [Secondary Mag](https://www.secondarymag.com/p/46-your-group-chat-is-a-37m-problem)
- Posh system failure and unresponsive support cost one organizer thousands [anecdote] — [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/posh.vip)

### Inferences
- NYC promoters' key jobs are promoter-level attribution (tracked links), SMS reach, fast payout, flexible door lists, and a buyer flow without app friction. Posh built for this and DICE partially conflicts with it.

### Gaps
- No r/nightlife or r/Promoters NYC threads were retrieved. Check-in app reliability complaints for NYC specifically: none found.

## Q4. What platforms advertise to organizers as differentiators

### Takeaway
The pitches cluster into five buckets: (1) speed of money (same-day or instant payouts, advances, financing), (2) fee position (0% merchant fee, flat fee, "free to publish," all-in pricing), (3) audience (discovery apps with millions of monthly fans, SMS blasts), (4) data and relationship tools (analytics, CRM, loyalty, resale royalties), and (5) nightlife-specific features (tables, promoter networks).

### Cited Findings
- KYD: same-day payouts, 0% merchant fees, resale royalties, loyalty, venue financing — [KYD](https://kydlabs.com/)
- Posh: 24–48h free payouts or 5% instant, free unlimited SMS, Kickbacks promoter network, Explore feed — [Posh platform](https://posh.vip/platform); [hi.events](https://hi.events/compare/dice-vs-posh)
- Shotgun: 5M+ monthly genre- and location-targeted fans — [Shotgun Pro](https://pro.shotgun.live/en)
- DICE: data helps promoters pick when, where and what to charge; anti-touting; all-in pricing — [Trapital](https://www.trapital.com/episodes/how-dice-makes-ticketing-more-fan-focused)
- Eventbrite: free to publish events; Instant Payouts product — [Eventbrite blog](https://www.eventbrite.com/blog/free-to-publish-events/); [Instant Payouts](https://www.eventbrite.com/help/en-us/articles/360444/get-paid-with-instant-payouts/)
- Humanitix: profits go to charity; nonprofit rate — [Humanitix](https://humanitix.com/us/pricing)
- Ticket Tailor: flat fee, own payment processor, white-label — [Toolradar](https://toolradar.com/tools/ticket-tailor)
- Tixr: VIP/table upsells, fan lifecycle — [Ticket Fairy](https://www.ticketfairy.com/event-ticketing/eventbrite-vs-tixr)
- Enterprise players (TM, AXS, SeatGeek, See Tickets): advances and signing bonuses — [Billboard (snippet)](https://www.billboard.com/pro/ticketmaster-alternatives-ticketing-company-competitors/)

### Cross-cutting themes (synthesis with citations above)
- **Payout holds, reserves and timing.** Eventbrite (3 days post-event, reported 20% reserve, cancelled payouts), Humanitix (on its schedule) vs Posh (24–48h) and KYD (same day). This is the #1 cash-flow pain point.
- **Account freezes near event date.** Eventbrite lockouts days before events [aggregator].
- **Fee changes and trust.** Eventbrite 2023 hikes and plan churn; Posh drip-pricing settlement; RA gradually adding service fees.
- **Data lock-in.** DICE's forced accounts and closed platform; TM contracts restricting fan data.
- **Support quality.** Posh anecdote; Eventbrite 1.2 on Trustpilot; Ticket Tailor praised.
- **App requirements.** DICE app-only.
- **Security.** See Tickets' 2023 skimmer.
- **Consolidation risk.** DICE to Fever (2025), Eventbrite to Bending Spoons (2026).
- **Exclusivity and advances.** TM/NIVA exclusivity, DOJ settlement multi-vendor terms, April 2026 verdict.
- **Check-in app reliability.** No specific citable evidence found (gap).

### Inferences
- An Exos-type entrant can credibly differentiate on transparent all-in pricing, fast payouts without large reserves, organizer-owned buyer data, and no app requirement for buyers, since each targets a documented incumbent pain point.

### Gaps
- Organizer-side check-in or scanner app reliability reviews for any platform: not found.
- Current AXS and Universe self-serve terms: not found.
