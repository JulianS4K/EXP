# Fan needs on the experience side: buying and attending live events (excluding price, fees and fairness)

Scope: US focus with NYC nightlife in mind, plus UK/EU where the evidence is strong, 2023–2026. Method: about 20 web searches and fetch attempts. The egress proxy blocked direct fetches of eventbrite.com, nme.com, easol.com and recordoftheday.com, so most evidence below comes from search-result snippets and is cited to the URL the snippet came from. Evidence strength is marked as follows. **[survey]** means a named survey with a sample size. **[vendor]** means a company claim or a case study. **[aggregator]** means a stats-roundup site of weak provenance. **[anecdote]** means individual reviews.

---

## Ranked fan needs (synthesis, experience side)

1. **Hearing about the event at all (discovery through social media and friends).** Unawareness is a bigger loss than friction.
2. **Fast, low-friction mobile checkout.** This means guest or no-forced-account checkout and Apple/Google Pay.
3. **Reliable entry on a phone ticket.** Wallet pass, working barcode, a fallback when the phone is dead, and no forced app download.
4. **Easy transfer to friends, plus group coordination.** Fans need to send tickets without cutoffs, reassign them and pay together.
5. **Reachable, human customer support,** especially for refunds and failed entry.
6. **Trust that the ticket is real.** Open marketplaces like Posh get fake-event complaints.
7. **Accessible booking and access information,** for the 80% of disabled fans who hit booking problems.
8. **Waitlists or face-value resale for sold-out shows,** and reminders.
9. **Social features (seeing where friends are going),** which matter most to nightlife and Gen Z users.

## 1. What do surveys and reports say? (discovery, Gen Z, mobile, social)

### Takeaway
Young fans find events mainly through Instagram, TikTok and friends. Being unaware of an event is a major reason tickets go unsold. Gen Z wants spontaneity, community and more events, and more of them go alone than before. Mobile tickets are close to the default expectation, but the precise preference percentages come mostly from weak aggregators.

### Cited Findings
**Discovery**
- **[vendor/survey]** Eventbrite says 30% of Gen Z buyers discover events on TikTok, 48% on Instagram and 24% by word of mouth. This comes from a search snippet summarising Eventbrite's stats roundup. A second search confirmed "almost 30% of Gen Z use TikTok for event discovery" but could not confirm the 48% Instagram figure. — [Eventbrite event statistics](https://www.eventbrite.com/blog/event-statistics-ds00/); [Eventbrite social media marketing blog](https://www.eventbrite.com/blog/how-to-promote-event-social-media-ds00/)
- **[survey]** Edison Research 2024, as summarised by a third party: 40% of US music followers use Instagram for music discovery and 36% use TikTok. Among 12–34-year-olds the figures are 63% and 58%. — [Dynamoi summary](https://dynamoi.com/learn/instagram-ads/instagram-reels-music-discovery-statistics)
- **[survey, provenance unclear]** 60% of event-goers aged 18–24 discover new artists through audio on TikTok or Instagram Reels. 51% have bought a ticket to see an artist they discovered that way. — search snippet; the likely origin is [Easol festival stats](https://easol.com/muse/26-music-festival-stats-2026/) or [DAC](https://www.dacgroup.com/insights/blog/paid-media/the-influence-of-music-on-social-media-trends-and-consumer-impact/). The underlying original survey was not verified.
- **[vendor]** Bandsintown reports 100M registered users and about 20M monthly click-throughs to ticketing pages. It says more than half of its users have attended concerts because of platform suggestions. It also claims that roughly 40% of US concert tickets go unsold each year, largely because fans don't know the event is happening. — [Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/live-music-discovery-platform-bandsintown-reaches-100m-registered-users/); [Billboard on Bandsintown research](https://www.billboard.com/music/music-news/bandsintown-concert-audience-research-fans-need-to-be-informed-1084033/). The 40% figure is a vendor claim, and the Billboard piece may predate 2023.
- **[vendor]** Bandsintown expanded its presale features, including fan presale alerts, in November 2024. — [Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/2024/11/14/bandsintown-expands-ticket-presale-features/)

**Gen Z and nightlife**
- **[survey]** Eventbrite "Reset to Real" Social Study, published January 2026. It surveyed 4,051 people aged 18–35 in the US and UK in July 2025:
  - 79% plan to attend more events in 2026.
  - 79% value spontaneity over a perfect plan.
  - Nearly 9 in 10 want events that connect them to community.
  - 58% prefer events where socialising isn't the main focus.
  - It recommends discovery through micro-influencers, past attendees and local creators.
  - — [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260114088488/en/Eventbrites-Inaugural-Social-Study-Report-Reveals-the-Reset-to-Real-How-Gen-Z-and-Millennials-Are-Redefining-Live-Experiences-in-2026); [Eventbrite IR](https://investor.eventbrite.com/press-releases/press-releases-details/2026/Eventbrites-Inaugural-Social-Study-Report-Reveals-the-Reset-to-Real-How-Gen-Z-and-Millennials-Are-Redefining-Live-Experiences-in-2026/default.aspx)
- **[vendor]** Eventbrite's "soft clubbing" nightlife release says 1 in 5 US adults actively seek alcohol-free events. — [Eventbrite newsroom](https://www.eventbrite.com/blog/press/newsroom/the-new-nightlife-gen-zs-soft-clubbing/) (fetch was blocked; the figure comes from the snippet)
- **[vendor]** Eventbrite's January 2025 "Fourth Spaces" report describes Gen Z and Millennials bridging their digital and physical worlds. — [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250128081264/en/Eventbrites-New-Report-Reveals-Emergence-of-Fourth-Spaces-As-Gen-Z-and-Millennials-Look-to-Bridge-Their-Digital-and-Physical-Worlds)
- **[survey]** Live Nation "Living for Live" 2025 surveyed 40,000 people aged 18–54 in 15 countries:
  - 93% attend shows because they crave real experiences.
  - 80% would rather spend on experiences than on things.
  - Live music is the #1 entertainment choice (39%).
  - — [Live Nation newsroom](https://news.livenationentertainment.com/news/global-report-surveying-40000-people-finds-live-music-is-the-worlds-favorite-form-of-entertainment); [Digital Music News](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2025/11/11/live-nation-global-survey-live-music/)
- **[survey]** Luminate: Gen Z is the only US generation where a higher share of women than men attended a concert in every quarter from Q1 2023 to Q1 2025. The share of Gen Z attending two concerts a year rose from 29% (Q1 2024) to 37% (Q1 2026). — [Luminate](https://luminatedata.com/blog/how-gen-z-defies-live-music-audience-trends/)
- **[vendor]** A Ticketmaster report says 29% of festival-goers attended alone in 2025, against 8% in 2019. — via search snippet referencing [Eventnoire](https://blog.eventnoire.com/millennials-gen-z-solo-festival-travel/). The original Ticketmaster report was not located.

**Mobile tickets**
- **[aggregator, low confidence]** "86% of ticket buyers prefer mobile delivery" (2024). "78% prefer mobile ticketing apps" (2023). "70% of 18–34 concertgoers want to use their phone as a ticket." — [Softjourn ticketing stats](https://softjourn.com/insights/ticketing-stats); [Gitnux](https://gitnux.org/ticketing-industry-statistics/). The original sources are unknown, so treat these as directional only.

### Inferences
- Discovery works mostly through social platforms and friends. A ticketing product for NYC nightlife should make event pages easy to share on Instagram and TikTok, with rich link previews, rather than rely on its own marketplace for discovery.
- The rise in going alone (29%), together with Gen Z valuing spontaneity (79%), argues for fast last-minute purchase and for social features that fit both solo and group attendance.

### Gaps
- I found no primary data on Apple Wallet adoption for tickets, such as the share of buyers who add passes to their wallet.
- I found no primary Spotify discovery data for ticket purchases. Spotify's concert listings and Live Events feed have no public conversion statistics.
- I found no data on RA or DICE as discovery sources (share of discovery) in the US.
- I found no clean Gen Z vs older generation split on discovery channels, beyond Edison's 12–34 cut.

## 2. What do fans praise and complain about for each platform?

### Takeaway
Across platforms the same complaints recur: unreachable customer support and slow refunds, transfer restrictions and failures, forced app downloads (DICE), and fake events on open platforms (Posh). The praise is for ease of use, discovery and a good feel for the local scene (DICE, Shotgun). All of this is **[anecdote]**, and review sites skew negative.

### Cited Findings
- **DICE.**
  - Praised for ease of finding and booking tickets.
  - Complaints:
    - The app is required, and fans without an account are forced to download it.
    - Some venues need yet another app.
    - Transfers only work to other DICE users, with cutoff times before the show. One user said the send-to-friend option was disabled on show day and they lost the second ticket.
    - No refunds; the only option is transferring the ticket yourself.
    - Customer service rarely replies, and SMS codes go missing.
  - — [Trustpilot dice.fm](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/dice.fm); [Trustpilot p.2](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/dice.fm?page=2); [AppPicker review](https://www.apppicker.com/music/dice-live-shows); [PissedConsumer](https://www.pissedconsumer.com/dice-fm/RT-F.html)
- **Posh (posh.vip).**
  - Buyers report tickets rejected at the door with "this ticket doesn't exist."
  - Anyone can create events without verification ("a playground for people who want to sell fake tickets").
  - A refund sat at "reviewed" for about 2 months.
  - There is no phone, chat or escalation path, only slow emails and AI responses.
  - — [Trustpilot posh.vip](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/posh.vip); [App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/posh-create-find-events/id1556928106?see-all=reviews&platform=iphone)
- **Resident Advisor.** Mixed reviews: complaints about refunds, unresponsive email and limited support. Fans value RA's face-value resale queue, where resale tickets only appear once the event is sold out, and its queueing for peak on-sales. — [Trustpilot ra.co](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/ra.co); [RA resale help](https://support.ra.co/article/93-resale); [RA queue help](https://support.ra.co/article/49-queueit)
- **Shotgun.**
  - Praised as "finally an app that actually understands the electronic scene and makes buying tickets painless," with a real community feel, artist previews, genre and vibe tags, easy URL sharing and cashback.
  - Complaints: fees, crashes and unresponsive support.
  - Features include seeing where friends are going, ambassador rewards, resale and waitlists for sold-out events. It operates in the US, France, the UK and elsewhere.
  - — [App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/shotgun-live-music-experience/id760028892?see-all=reviews); [Nevent overview](https://nevent.ai/en/ticketing-platforms/shotgun/)
- **Ticketmaster.**
  - SafeTix rotating barcodes block screenshots, and passes in Apple/Google Wallet hide the barcode.
  - Fans report "Pass disabled" errors and barcodes that don't appear at the venue.
  - One Trustpilot reviewer tried a transfer three times, waited two hours for support, and was told "sometimes ticket transfers don't work."
  - — [Ticketmaster SafeTix](https://www.ticketmaster.com/safetix); [TM help: Wallet not working](https://help.ticketmaster.com/hc/en-us/articles/14296188194449-My-ticket-isn-t-working-in-my-Apple-Wallet-What-should-I-do); [NeatPass](https://neatpass.app/learn/ticketmaster-apple-wallet-not-scanning); [Trustpilot Ticketmaster](https://ie.trustpilot.com/review/ticketmaster.com)

### Inferences
- Nightlife fans see support failures and transfer lock-outs as the core failure modes. Both are fixable product decisions: allow late transfers, and provide a human or SLA-backed support path.
- Posh's complaints show the downside of open event creation. Verifying organizers, or showing trust signals on the page, meets a real fan need.

### Gaps
- I found no structured, quantified review analysis, such as the share of 1-star reviews by theme, for any platform.
- AXS, SeatGeek, StubHub and Eventbrite buyer reviews were not specifically retrieved within the tool budget.
- The Reddit threads (r/aves, r/nyc, r/DICE) were not reachable through search. No Reddit evidence is included.

## 3. Which features correlate with conversion?

### Takeaway
The strongest general-ecommerce evidence says that forced account creation and long checkouts cost roughly a fifth of would-be buyers each, and that express wallets such as Apple Pay raise mobile conversion substantially. I found no public case study specific to ticketing.

### Cited Findings
- **[survey]** Baymard (2025): average cart abandonment is 70.22% across 50 studies. Among US shoppers who abandoned for a reason other than browsing:
  - 39% cited extra costs (this is the price topic, out of scope).
  - 19% cited mandatory account creation.
  - 18% said checkout was too long or complicated.
  - 19% did not trust the site with their card.
  - — [Baymard cart abandonment](https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate); [Baymard: reduce abandonment](https://baymard.com/blog/reduce-cart-abandonment)
- **[vendor]** Stripe: businesses see on average a 2x conversion increase when Apple Pay is offered through the Express Checkout Element early in the flow, compared with showing it at the end. Wish saw a 2x higher conversion lift by defaulting Apple Pay-ready new users to Apple Pay. — [Stripe Apple Pay best practices](https://stripe.com/docs/apple-pay/best-practices); [Stripe payment methods test](https://stripe.com/blog/testing-the-conversion-impact-of-50-plus-global-payment-methods)
- **[vendor]** Xola, a bookings platform, reports that Apple Pay and Google Pay lift mobile booking conversion. — [Xola](https://www.xola.com/articles/how-apple-pay-and-google-pay-can-lift-mobile-conversion-rates/)
- **Group and social products.**
  - SeatGeek "Parties" (August 2024): the owner sends one invite link, guests claim tickets into their own accounts, and the host can revoke and reassign.
  - DICE "Groups": discover shows, find friends and buy together.
  - StubHub published a 2026 guide on coordinating friends and payments.
  - — [Digital Music News](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2024/08/22/seatgeek-ticket-sharing-management-features/); [TicketNews](https://www.ticketnews.com/2024/08/seatgeek-reveals-new-parties-feature-for-group-ticketing/); [Record of the Day (DICE Groups)](https://www.recordoftheday.com/news-and-press/dice-launches-new-product-featuregroupsto-help-fans-attend-more-live-events-with-friends); [StubHub newsroom](https://newsroom.stubhub.com/2026/07/30/group-ticket-buying-how-to-coordinate-friends-and-payments/)
- **Split payment.** Ticketmaster doesn't support splitting a payment across cards. Third parties such as Kasheesh offer workarounds. — [Kasheesh](https://www.kasheesh.co/finance-academy/split-payment-pay-with-two-cards-on-ticketmaster/)

### Inferences
- For Exos, the best-supported conversion levers are guest checkout (email plus Apple/Google Pay, no password) and putting Apple Pay first on mobile.
- "Pay your own share" group checkout is a gap: major platforms handle ticket distribution (Parties, Groups) but not split payment. That makes it a plausible differentiator, but no conversion data supports it yet.

### Gaps
- I found no public data on whether social proof, such as "X friends going" or "selling fast," lifts ticket conversion.
- I found no ticketing-specific data on checkout drop-off or time to purchase.

## 4. Accessibility, the door experience, support and app requirements

### Takeaway
Disabled fans report widespread booking barriers (UK data is strongest). At the door, the need is reliable scanning with a fallback when the phone is dead or the pass is disabled. There is anecdotal but consistent resentment of forced app downloads.

### Cited Findings
- **[survey]** Attitude is Everything, 4th State of Access report (UK, early 2024, n=349 Deaf and disabled fans):
  - 80% had problems booking tickets.
  - 79% had been put off buying because of inaccessible booking systems.
  - 73% felt discriminated against.
  - 37% felt things had improved.
  - 1 in 10 had considered legal action.
  - The report launched the "Ticketing Without Barriers" coalition.
  - — [AIE State of Access reports](https://attitudeiseverything.org.uk/all-resources/state-of-access-reports/); [Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/pro/deaf-disabled-fans-uk-live-music-study-attitude-everything/)
- **[news]** Disabled fans welcomed Ticketmaster changes that simplify accessible ticket access in the UK (details are unverified because the fetch was blocked). — [NME](https://www.nme.com/news/music/disabled-music-fans-welcome-changes-by-ticketmaster-to-simplify-ticket-access-2563165)
- **[survey]** ESPN surveyed venues and found they fall short on invisible-disability needs (sports context). — [ESPN](https://africa.espn.com/espn/story/_/id/41016241/espn-survey-venues-fall-short-invisible-disability-needs)
- **Door experience.** Rotating barcodes such as SafeTix block screenshots. Wallet-pass failures ("Pass disabled," barcode missing) are a documented support topic. — [Ticketmaster SafeTix](https://www.ticketmaster.com/safetix); [TM help](https://help.ticketmaster.com/hc/en-us/articles/14296188194449-My-ticket-isn-t-working-in-my-Apple-Wallet-What-should-I-do)
- **App requirement [anecdote].** DICE reviewers complain about being forced to download the app. — [AppPicker](https://www.apppicker.com/music/dice-live-shows); [Trustpilot DICE](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/dice.fm)

### Inferences
- A web ticket that works without an app, plus a wallet pass, plus a door-side lookup by name or ID for dead phones, addresses several of the complaints above at once. Exos's offline check-in design fits this.
- Accessibility needs are concrete: an accessible-booking path without phoning in, clear venue access information, and companion tickets. The UK evidence is strong, but I found no equivalent US nightlife survey.

### Gaps
- I found no quantitative data on entry wait times, or on how often fans are turned away because of a dead phone.
- I found no US survey equivalent to AIE on disabled fans' ticketing, and no data on ADA complaints specific to NYC clubs.
- I found no survey on waitlist or reminder usage or value. The only evidence is feature existence (Shotgun waitlists, RA resale queue, Bandsintown alerts).
- I found no survey on app versus web preference by age.
